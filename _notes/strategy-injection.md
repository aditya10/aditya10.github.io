Small VLMs are quite capable at tool use. I am working on a problem where we want to use tool-use agentic VLMs to study videos generated by T2V models, and identify errors in them. I provide the model with tools for viewing frames from videos at higher fps, showing frames side-by-side for comparisons, zooming into different frame regions, and even optical flow based frame motion comparison.

{% include fn/margin.html label="Side note" text="see this Reddit post about how bad Qwen’s thinking is" %}
Here’s something interesting I found: models like Qwen 3.5 VL 9B are absolutely capable of tool-use, but they are hampered by their inability to reason about the problem well, come up with hypotheses to test, and strategically use tools.

## Research Question

If a bigger model, say Opus 5.5 or GPT-6 Sol, could provide the Qwen model with a strategy, can this strategy be injected into Qwen’s multi-turn conversation to improve tool use?

{% include fn/figure.html src="/assets/notes/strategy-injection/strategy-injection-01.webp" alt="Diagram titled “Large VLM Teacher strategy injected into Qwen 3.5 VL conversation”. A video and prompt lead into a Strategy step, produced by a large VLM and injected into the conversation, with three parts: set the scene, generate hypotheses to validate, and set up a tool-use plan. Strategy leads to a looping Tool Use step and then Answer Submission, along an arrow labelled multi-turn conversation. Listed Qwen-3.5-VL 9B tool-use outcomes: reasoning is succinct as it tries to follow the injected style, and tool use directly targets error regions based on the plan." %}

The short answer is **YES**! In fact, it produced some rather interesting outcomes… not only did Qwen become more accurate at tool-use, but its reasoning traces on the video were shorter, more succinct and more confident. This side effect is interesting: by modifying the expectation of how the Qwen model should think about the video, it started adapting that thinking setting to all future turns.
